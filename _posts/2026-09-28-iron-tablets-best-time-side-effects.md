The best time to take iron tablets is in the morning, on an empty stomach, with a glass of water. Keep tea, coffee, milk and calcium away from the dose, because they reduce how much iron your body absorbs. Newer research shows that splitting iron into several doses a day doesn't help, and that taking it every other day lets you absorb more of each dose. If an empty stomach makes you feel sick, taking your tablet with a little food is better than stopping.

## How timing and drinks change absorption

A [2023 randomised crossover trial](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/43560916-822e-421a-b387-f1f9a0e385f5/content) gave 34 women with low iron stores a 100 mg dose of iron (as ferrous fumarate) under different conditions. It then measured the share of iron each woman absorbed.

| How the iron was taken | Share of iron absorbed |
|---|---|
| With water, in the morning, fasting | 20.6% |
| With 80 mg vitamin C | 26.7% |
| With 500 mg vitamin C | 30.5% (no significant gain over 80 mg) |
| With coffee | 9.5% |
| With breakfast and coffee | 6.9% |
| In the afternoon | 12.9% |

Coffee cut absorption by 54%. Breakfast with coffee cut it by 66%. An afternoon dose absorbed 37% less than a morning one. The researchers found that taking iron with orange juice instead of with breakfast gave about four times the absorption.

## Why the morning works best

Your body controls iron intake with a hormone called hepcidin. When hepcidin is high, your gut lets less iron through.

Hepcidin follows a daily rhythm. In the same trial, it was [lower in the morning than in the afternoon](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/43560916-822e-421a-b387-f1f9a0e385f5/content), which helps explain why morning doses absorb better.

A dose of iron also raises hepcidin by itself. Even in women with iron-deficiency anaemia, an oral dose [triggers a rise in hepcidin that lasts about 24 hours](https://haematologica.org/article/view/9379). So a second tablet taken later the same day, or early the next morning, meets a gut that is less willing to absorb it.

The trials tested taking iron at the same time as food or drinks. They didn't test an exact gap before breakfast. Taking your tablet when you wake up and eating a little later is a sensible habit.

## Once a day or every other day?

Several trials have looked at how often to take iron.

- In [iron-depleted women taking 60 mg](https://www.consultant360.com/story/iron-absorption-better-alternate-day-dosing), the share absorbed was 16.3% with daily dosing and 21.8% with alternate-day dosing. Total iron absorbed was 131.0 mg versus 175.3 mg.
- In the [same set of trials](https://www.consultant360.com/story/iron-absorption-better-alternate-day-dosing), splitting 120 mg into two 60 mg doses a day made no significant difference to absorption compared with one 120 mg dose.
- In [women with iron-deficiency anaemia](https://haematologica.org/article/view/9379), doses taken with no dose the day before were absorbed 40–50% better than a dose taken on the following day.

The [British Society of Gastroenterology (BSG) 2021 guideline](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) accepts that alternate-day dosing improves absorption. It also notes that 60 mg twice a day raised haemoglobin faster than 120 mg on alternate days, although alternate-day dosing reached a similar rise once the same total dose had been taken, with less nausea. Its recommendation is one tablet a day, such as one ferrous sulfate 200 mg tablet, taken on an empty stomach, with alternate days as an option.

Some product leaflets still give older advice. A [current UK ferrous sulfate leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil) says one tablet two or three times a day for treatment. Your doctor may also have prescribed more than one tablet a day for a reason specific to you. Follow the dose you were given. If you think once a day or alternate days might suit you better, ask your doctor or pharmacist before you change anything.

## What to keep away from your iron tablet

**Tea and coffee.** Coffee taken with iron [halved absorption or worse](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/43560916-822e-421a-b387-f1f9a0e385f5/content). An older study of iron from meals found black tea [cut absorption by 79–94%](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/inhibition-of-nonhaem-iron-absorption-in-man-by-polyphenoliccontaining-beverages/5CFF5FF58F6CBA86D3646981A7DF0418), and adding milk to the tea made little difference. This includes karak chai and Arabic coffee. Have them later in the day, away from your tablet.

**Milk, eggs and dairy.** The [ferrous sulfate leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil) says not to take iron with tea, milk or eggs.

**Calcium, magnesium and antacids.** The same leaflet says to leave at least 2 hours between iron and antacids or mineral supplements such as calcium and magnesium. If you take [magnesium](/blog/magnesium-types-what-the-evidence-shows/) or calcium, take them at a different time of day. Check the labels of other supplements too, including [vitamin D](/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-uae/) products and multivitamins, for added calcium or iron.

**Stomach acid medicines.** People taking omeprazole have been reported to [respond poorly to ferrous sulfate](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21150767/), and a [case series](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jha2.96) describes iron deficiency caused by this type of medicine. Spacing the doses doesn't solve this, because acid suppression lasts all day. If you take one long term and your iron isn't improving, tell your doctor.

## Medicines to space apart from iron

Iron binds to several medicines in the gut, which can stop one or both from being absorbed properly. These are the spacing rules from the sources below.

| Medicine | How to space it from iron |
|---|---|
| Tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics, chloramphenicol | At least 2 hours before or after ([leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil)) |
| Bisphosphonates for bone health | At least 2 hours before or after ([leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil)) |
| Antacids, calcium and magnesium | At least 2 hours before or after ([leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil)) |
| Levothyroxine | At least 4 hours apart ([Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/drug-interactions/iron-sulfate-with-levothyroxine-1082-13326-1463-0.html)) |
| Dolutegravir | Take it 2 hours before or 6 hours after iron, or take both together with food ([Liverpool HIV Interactions](https://hiv-druginteractions.org/interactions/120370)) |

The leaflet also lists methyldopa, penicillamine, colestyramine and zinc supplements as interacting. Some antibiotic leaflets ask for a longer gap than 2 hours. Show your pharmacist everything you take, and ask them to set the timings for you.

## Iron tablet side effects and how common they are

A [2015 meta-analysis of 43 trials in 6,831 adults](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0117383) found that ferrous sulfate more than doubled the odds of stomach side effects compared with placebo (odds ratio 2.32). In people taking ferrous sulfate, the pooled rates were:

- constipation, about 12%
- nausea, about 11%
- diarrhoea, about 8%.

The same analysis found no clear link between dose and side effects, and no evidence that modified-release (slow-release) products reduce them. A [separate trial](https://haematologica.org/article/view/9379) found no significant difference in stomach side effects between 100 mg and 200 mg. The evidence isn't fully settled, though. An [NHS formulary table](https://handbook.ggcmedicines.org.uk/media/0gkhwxhm/elemental-iron-content-of-oral-iron-preparations.pdf) still discourages 100 mg elemental iron products because of side effects.

The [leaflet](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil) also lists stomach pain and dark stools. Chewing or sucking the tablet can cause mouth ulcers, so swallow it whole with water.

**Dark or black stools** are an expected, harmless effect of iron. Get urgent medical help if you vomit blood, have severe stomach pain, or feel faint or dizzy and have black, tarry stools.

## How to make side effects easier

- **Take one tablet a day** if that is what you were prescribed. If you were told to take more and you're struggling, ask your doctor whether a lower dose would suit you.
- **Ask about alternate days.** The [BSG guideline](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) lists it as an option for people who can't tolerate iron, and it caused less nausea.
- **Take it with a little food** if an empty stomach makes you sick. You'll absorb less, as the breakfast figures in the table above show, but some iron is better than none.
- **Help constipation** with plenty of water and fibre. Your pharmacist can suggest a suitable laxative if you need one.
- **Don't just swap brands.** The [BSG guideline](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) says switching from one traditional iron salt to another isn't supported by evidence. For real intolerance, it lists alternate-day dosing, a newer form called ferric maltol, and iron given by drip. Your doctor can tell you which of these are available to you.

## Iron salts and elemental iron

The number on the box is usually the weight of the iron salt. What matters is the elemental iron, which is the amount of actual iron in each tablet. These figures come from an [NHS formulary table](https://handbook.ggcmedicines.org.uk/media/0gkhwxhm/elemental-iron-content-of-oral-iron-preparations.pdf).

| Product | Elemental iron |
|---|---|
| Ferrous sulfate 200 mg tablet | 65 mg |
| Ferrous fumarate 210 mg tablet | 68 mg |
| Ferrous fumarate 322 mg tablet | 100 mg |
| Ferrous fumarate 305 mg capsule | 100 mg |
| Ferrous gluconate 300 mg tablet | 35 mg |

When you compare products, look for the elemental iron figure on the label. The [BSG guideline](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) describes the traditional salts (sulfate, fumarate and gluconate) as inexpensive, effective and safe, and they remain the standard treatment. A plain, lower-cost iron tablet does the job, as with many [generic medicines](/blog/generic-vs-brand-medicines-uae/).

You'll also see ferrous bisglycinate on UAE shelves. A [2023 review of 17 trials](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36728680/), 9 of them in pregnancy, reported fewer stomach side effects with bisglycinate in pregnant women. Many of the trials were small, the authors called for more research, and the finding was [publicised by a bisglycinate manufacturer](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/in-pregnant-women-ferrous-bisglycinate-supplements-may-be-more-effective-and-have-lower-risk-of-side-effects-than-iron-salts-new-review-shows). The evidence is promising but still limited.

## How long to take iron, and when to recheck your blood

The [BSG guideline](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) gives clear markers for whether iron is working.

- A rise in haemoglobin of at least 10 g/L (1 g/dL) after 2 weeks is a good sign. If it hasn't risen by that much, treatment is likely to fail.
- A rise of 20 g/L (2 g/dL), or a return to the normal range, by 4 weeks counts as an adequate response.
- Blood should be checked within the first 4 weeks, then about every 4 weeks until haemoglobin is normal.
- Once haemoglobin is normal, keep taking iron for around 3 months to refill your stores.

So don't stop when you start to feel better. The same guideline stresses that iron deficiency always has a cause, and it recommends checking the gut in men and in women after menopause.

## Keep iron away from children

US labelling rules require iron products to carry a warning that accidental iron overdose is ["a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6"](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/small-entity-compliance-guide-label-warning-statements-iron-containing-supplements-and-drugs). Coated tablets can look like sweets. Store all iron, including pregnancy vitamins, out of sight and reach of children. If a child swallows any, take them to the nearest emergency department straight away, even if they seem well.

## Iron and thalassaemia trait in the UAE

Anaemia is common here. A [survey of 654 UAE university students](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1780083/full) found anaemia in 60.7% of the women, based on haemoglobin alone. Not all anaemia is iron deficiency.

Thalassaemia trait is also common. In [Ras Al Khaimah premarital screening from 2008 to 2015](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201620855543817.page), 2.98% of 17,826 people tested carried beta-thalassaemia trait. Premarital screening has been [mandatory for all couples since 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0170485), so many people already know their status.

Thalassaemia trait can look like iron deficiency on a basic blood count, because both give small red cells. Iron won't fix it. That is why a ferritin test, which measures your iron stores, matters before you take iron long term. People with iron storage conditions such as haemochromatosis [should not take iron](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/3130/pil) at all.

## Questions people ask

**Can I take iron at night?**
You can, but you'll probably absorb less. In the trial above, an afternoon dose absorbed [37% less than a morning dose](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/43560916-822e-421a-b387-f1f9a0e385f5/content). A time you'll remember and can tolerate still beats a missed dose.

**Should I take vitamin C with iron?**
Vitamin C raised absorption from a single dose [by about 30%](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/43560916-822e-421a-b387-f1f9a0e385f5/content). A [trial of 440 adults with iron-deficiency anaemia](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2772395), however, found that adding vitamin C made no real difference to haemoglobin recovery (a rise of 4.20 versus 4.07 g/dL at 8 weeks) or to side effects. The [BSG](https://www.gamedii.com.br/docs/consensos/bsg/Iron-Deficiency-Aneamia-in-Adults-gutjnl-2021-325210.pdf) doesn't recommend adding it routinely. A glass of orange juice with your tablet is fine, and you don't need to buy vitamin C tablets.

**I'm pregnant. How much iron should I take?**
Follow the plan your antenatal team gives you. Many pregnancy vitamins already contain iron, so check the labels to avoid taking a double dose.

**Why are my stools black?**
Dark or black stools are a normal, harmless effect of iron. Seek urgent help if you also vomit blood, have severe stomach pain, or feel faint or dizzy.
