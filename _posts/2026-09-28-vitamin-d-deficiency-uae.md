Most people with low vitamin D have no symptoms at all. When deficiency is severe or has lasted a long time, the symptoms with solid evidence are bone pain and tenderness, muscle weakness in the hips and thighs, and rickets in children. Tiredness, hair loss and low mood are often blamed on low vitamin D, yet supplement trials have mostly not shown improvement in them. A blood test for 25-hydroxyvitamin D confirms the diagnosis.

Low vitamin D is very common in the UAE, even with year-round sunshine. A [2025 systematic review of 35 UAE studies](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1604819/full) found that adults averaged 17.6 ng/mL (about 44 nmol/L), below the 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) level most guidelines treat as adequate. This guide explains which symptoms matter, when a test is worth doing, and how much vitamin D to take.

## How common is vitamin D deficiency in the UAE?

Every UAE study finds that most people tested have low levels. The exact percentage depends on the cut-off each study used, so each figure below comes with its threshold.

| Study | Who was tested | Cut-off used | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Yammine and Al Adham, Dubai, 2016](https://www.emro.who.int/emhj-volume-22-2016/volume-22-issue-9/the-status-of-serum-vitamin-d-in-the-population-of-the-united-arab-emirates.html) | 7,924 hospital patients | Below 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L) | 85.4% below the cut-off; only 2.1% at 50 ng/mL or above |
| [Al Zarooni et al., Abu Dhabi, 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0264064) | 392 Emirati adults | Below 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) | 79.6% below the cut-off |
| [UAE systematic review, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1604819/full) | 35 studies, 28,260 people | Pooled average | Adults averaged 17.6 ng/mL (about 44 nmol/L) |

Most of these people were tested at clinics and hospitals, so they may differ from the general population. The laboratory method used also explained a large share of the differences between studies.

## Why it is so common in a country this sunny

The UAE gets around 10 hours of sunshine a day. Your skin makes vitamin D when sunlight reaches it, and here very little sunlight reaches bare skin. UAE studies in the [2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1604819/full) point to several reasons.

- **Heat keeps people indoors.** One UAE study found that women's levels were lower in summer than in winter (8.4 against 12.5 ng/mL on average), because people avoid the sun when it is strongest.
- **Covering clothing.** Among university students, 37.5% of hijab wearers had low vitamin D, compared with 16.7% of those in Western dress. In Abu Dhabi, Emirati adults whose clothing exposed their arms or legs had [higher levels](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0264064). One comparison found Emirati women averaged 8.6 ng/mL while Europeans living in the UAE averaged 64.3 ng/mL.
- **Indoor, sedentary work.** Sedentary workers had lower levels than people in physically demanding jobs.
- **Darker skin.** People with darker skin are a recognised higher-risk group, according to the [US National Institutes of Health](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/).
- **Diet.** In the Abu Dhabi study, each extra serving of fish or eggs went with a level 2.4 nmol/L higher.
- **Weight.** The link with obesity is mixed. Of 13 UAE studies that looked at body weight, only 5 found a significant association.

Breastfed infants, older adults and people with conditions that reduce absorption from the gut are also at higher risk.

We could not confirm any mandatory UAE programme for adding vitamin D to staple foods. Fortifying milk has been [proposed since at least 2012](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/health/sunshine-vitamin-d-could-be-added-to-milk-in-the-uae-1.435157), so check the label if you want to know whether a milk or juice contains vitamin D.

## Symptoms that are well supported

Vitamin D helps your body absorb calcium and phosphate. The well-proven symptoms come from what happens to bones and muscles when that system fails over months or years.

| Who | Sign or symptom | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Children | Rickets | Bowed legs or knock knees, widened wrists, late walking, poor growth, late teeth, irritability |
| Infants and children | Low blood calcium | Muscle spasms and cramps, and in severe cases seizures |
| Adults | Osteomalacia (soft bones) | Deep, aching bone pain, often in the lower back, pelvis and legs; bones that hurt when pressed |
| Adults | Weakness in the large muscles of the hips and thighs | Difficulty climbing stairs or getting up from a chair; in severe cases a waddling walk |
| Older adults | Weaker bones | Low bone density and higher fracture risk, mainly when deficiency is severe |

Muscle weakness and falls in older adults, and general aches in muscles and bones, have a moderate, mostly observational link with low vitamin D.

A slightly low result on a routine test does not mean your bones are affected. In the Abu Dhabi study of Emirati adults, low vitamin D was [not linked with past fractures or osteoporosis](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0264064).

## Symptoms that are often claimed but weakly supported

Many online lists of "signs of low vitamin D" include problems the evidence supports poorly. Low vitamin D is so common in the UAE that it often turns up alongside these symptoms by chance.

- **Tiredness.** The Abu Dhabi study found a borderline link with fatigue, but a one-off survey cannot show cause. Supplement trials have given inconsistent results.
- **Low mood and depression.** Nine randomised trials in 4,923 adults found [no significant reduction in depressive symptoms](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/) with supplements. The depression study within the large VITAL trial also found no benefit.
- **General aches and chronic pain.** A [Cochrane review](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD007771.pub3/full) found no consistent evidence that vitamin D improves chronic pain in adults, and rated the evidence as low quality.
- **Hair loss.** Links with some types of hair loss come from observational studies only. There is no good trial evidence that vitamin D treats it.
- **Frequent infections.** The evidence is mixed. The [Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease) notes that supplements may reduce respiratory infections in children.

Tiredness and hair loss have many common causes, including iron deficiency, anaemia, thyroid problems and poor sleep. If tiredness is your main complaint, ask your doctor to check these too. Our guides to [iron tablets](/blog/iron-tablets-best-time-side-effects/) and [magnesium types](/blog/magnesium-types-what-the-evidence-shows/) cover two other supplements people often reach for.

## What vitamin D deficiency actually causes

Deficiency directly causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults. It can also cause secondary hyperparathyroidism, where the parathyroid glands become overactive, and it contributes to osteoporosis and fracture risk when deficiency is significant.

Low vitamin D has also been linked with heart disease, diabetes, depression, some cancers and autoimmune disease, mostly in observational studies. People who are unwell, less active or heavier tend to spend less time outdoors, which can make low vitamin D look like a cause when it is only a marker. Large trials have tested whether supplements prevent these conditions.

| Outcome | What the trials found |
|---|---|
| Fractures | In the VITAL trial, 2,000 IU a day did not reduce fractures in 25,871 people followed for a median of 5.3 years ([NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)) |
| Heart attack and stroke | No significant protection ([NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)) |
| Getting cancer | No reduction ([NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)) |
| Dying from cancer | Meta-analyses suggest about 12–13% fewer cancer deaths ([NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)) |
| Death from any cause | In the D-Health trial, 60,000 IU once a month for 5 years in 21,315 Australians made no difference to deaths (5.3% and 5.1% in the two groups) ([NHS SPS summary](https://www.medicinesresources.nhs.uk/the-d-health-trial-a-randomised-controlled-trial-of-the-effect-of-vitamin-d-on-mortality.html)) |
| Type 2 diabetes | The Endocrine Society suggests supplements for people with high-risk prediabetes, to reduce progression to diabetes ([Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease)) |

Most people in these trials were not deficient to begin with. A benefit in people who are truly deficient cannot be ruled out, which matters in the UAE, where deficiency is common.

## Testing: who should and should not test

The test is a blood test for 25-hydroxyvitamin D, often written as 25(OH)D.

The [Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease) recommends against routine testing in healthy adults, including people with darker skin or obesity. It also states that the levels which give specific health benefits have not been established in clinical trials.

In Abu Dhabi, the [DOH Thiqa coverage policy](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/EAB3C65E8B614555870BA764A495C2C2.ashx) (July 2022) covers testing for people with:

- bone disease, such as rickets, osteomalacia, osteopenia or osteoporosis
- chronic kidney disease, liver failure or malabsorption
- parathyroid, calcium or phosphate disorders
- multiple sclerosis, lupus (SLE) or systemic sclerosis
- obesity, in children or adults
- age over 65, with testing once a year
- regular use of anti-seizure medicines, glucocorticoid steroids, HIV medicines, antifungals or cholestyramine

Under the same policy, people under 65 with no symptoms and no risk factors are not eligible for routine testing. This is an Abu Dhabi policy, and other emirates set their own rules.

### What your result means

| 25(OH)D in nmol/L | In ng/mL | What it means (NIH and Abu Dhabi DOH) |
|---|---|---|
| Below 30 | Below 12 | Deficient; DOH policy advises treatment |
| 30 to 50 | 12 to 20 | Inadequate; DOH policy advises treatment if you have risk factors |
| 50 or above | 20 or above | Adequate for most people |
| Above 125 | Above 50 | Possible harm |

To convert, 1 ng/mL equals 2.5 nmol/L.

Your lab report may use a higher target. The Endocrine Society's 2011 guideline used 30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L), a threshold its 2024 guideline no longer endorses. A [2018 UAE expert consensus](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0960076016302576), which is not an official guideline, suggested 30–50 ng/mL. So 25 ng/mL may be flagged as low on your report yet count as adequate under NIH and DOH cut-offs.

If you start treatment, the DOH policy retests 12 weeks later, allows up to two tests a year until the goal is reached, and then once a year.

## How much vitamin D to take

The standard daily amounts come from the US National Academy of Medicine, as tabled by the [NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/).

| Age | Daily amount |
|---|---|
| Infants up to 12 months | 400 IU (10 mcg) |
| 1 to 70 years, including pregnancy and breastfeeding | 600 IU (15 mcg) |
| Over 70 | 800 IU (20 mcg) |

The upper limit for adults is 4,000 IU (100 mcg) a day. Limits for young children are lower.

The [Endocrine Society](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease) suggests supplements above these amounts for a few groups. The doses below are the average daily doses used in the trials behind its advice.

| Group | Average daily dose in the trials |
|---|---|
| Children and teenagers aged 1 to 18 | About 1,200 IU |
| Pregnancy | About 2,500 IU |
| Adults aged 75 and over | About 900 IU |
| Adults with high-risk prediabetes | About 3,500 IU |

For healthy adults aged 19 to 74, it recommends against routinely taking more than the standard daily amount.

For confirmed deficiency, the [Abu Dhabi DOH policy](https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/EAB3C65E8B614555870BA764A495C2C2.ashx) treats adults below 30 nmol/L with 50,000 IU once a week for 8 to 12 weeks, followed by 800 to 2,000 IU daily. For children it uses 2,000 IU daily for 6 to 12 weeks. For prevention, it points to an over-the-counter vitamin D supplement.

### Daily, weekly or one huge dose

UAE pharmacy shelves carry products from [400 IU to 50,000 IU](https://gulfnews.com/lifestyle/most-uae-residents-are-vitamin-d-deficient-can-you-just-take-supplements-without-a-test-1.500651781). Bigger is not better.

- **Daily, lower doses** are the preferred option. The Endocrine Society prefers daily, lower-dose vitamin D over non-daily, higher doses in people aged 50 and over.
- **Weekly high doses** have a place as a short treatment course. The DOH regimen uses 50,000 IU weekly for 8 to 12 weeks, then switches to a lower daily dose. Taking 50,000 IU every week indefinitely, without testing, is a different matter.
- **Very large single doses** have a poor track record. A trial that gave older women a [single high dose once a year](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/0003-4819-153-6-201009210-02003) found more falls and fractures. In D-Health, 60,000 IU once a month gave no survival benefit, and the authors cautioned against that regimen.

If you are offered a very large single dose, by tablet or injection, ask why a daily or weekly plan would not suit you.

## Who should be careful

Too much vitamin D raises the calcium in your blood. According to the [NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/), toxicity usually occurs at levels above 150 ng/mL (375 nmol/L) and can cause kidney damage and, in severe cases, heart rhythm problems. Reported symptoms include [nausea, thirst, passing more urine, constipation, confusion and kidney stones](https://gulfnews.com/lifestyle/most-uae-residents-are-vitamin-d-deficient-can-you-just-take-supplements-without-a-test-1.500651781).

Speak to a pharmacist or doctor before taking more than the standard daily amount if any of these apply.

- **You take a thiazide diuretic** (a type of water tablet often used for blood pressure). Thiazides reduce the calcium you lose in urine, which adds to the risk of high calcium.
- **You take orlistat, steroid tablets or a statin**, which can all interact with vitamin D.
- **You take anti-seizure medicines, HIV medicines, antifungals or cholestyramine**, which the DOH lists as reasons to test.
- **You have kidney disease, liver failure, malabsorption, or a parathyroid or calcium disorder.** These need testing and supervised treatment.
- **You take several products.** A multivitamin, a calcium-plus-D tablet and a separate vitamin D all count towards the 4,000 IU daily upper limit. Add them up.

## Questions people ask

### Can I get enough vitamin D from the UAE sun?

For many people, no. UAE studies consistently find low levels despite the sunshine, and summer levels can be lower than winter levels because the heat keeps people indoors.

### Should I take 50,000 IU a week without a test?

The Abu Dhabi DOH uses 50,000 IU weekly as an 8 to 12 week course for confirmed deficiency, followed by a lower daily dose. Staying on it long term without monitoring risks raising your calcium too high. A standard daily dose is the safer choice if you have not been tested.

### How long does it take to correct a deficiency?

The DOH treatment course lasts 8 to 12 weeks, with a repeat blood test at 12 weeks.

### Is vitamin D3 better than D2?

Yes. The [NIH](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/) notes that D3 raises blood levels more and keeps them up for longer than D2, so choose D3 if you have the option.
