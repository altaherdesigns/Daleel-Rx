> **If someone in the heat is confused, slurring their words, behaving strangely, having a seizure or has collapsed, treat it as heat stroke. Call 998 for an ambulance now.**
>
> Then move them somewhere cool, take off their outer clothing and cool them as fast as you can with cold water, wet cloths and fanning. Do not wait for the ambulance to start cooling. A person who is still sweating can still have heat stroke.

Dehydration, heat exhaustion and heat stroke sit on one spectrum, and the signs of heat stress change at each stage. Dehydration makes you thirsty and your urine dark. Heat exhaustion makes you sweaty, weak and dizzy, but you still think clearly and you should [feel better within 30 minutes of cooling down](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/). Heat stroke affects the brain. It is a medical emergency.

## Quick comparison

| | Dehydration | Heat exhaustion | Heat stroke |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Key signs** | Thirst, dark yellow strong-smelling urine, peeing less, dry mouth, headache, light-headedness, tiredness | Heavy sweating, pale clammy skin, dizziness, headache, nausea or vomiting, cramps, weakness, thirst. Thinking stays clear | Confusion, slurred speech, odd behaviour, seizure, collapse or unconsciousness, very high temperature. Skin may be hot and dry, or still sweating |
| **What to do** | Drink water. Use oral rehydration salts (ORS) if you are vomiting or have diarrhoea | Move somewhere cool, remove extra clothing, drink water or a rehydration drink, cool the skin | Call 998, then cool the person fast and keep cooling |
| **Emergency?** | Usually not. Get urgent help for confusion, severe drowsiness, cold or blotchy skin, or difficulty breathing | Not if clearly better within 30 minutes. Call 998 if there is no improvement or any confusion | Yes, always |

## Why UAE summers are different

The National Centre of Meteorology recorded [51.8°C at Sweihan, Al Ain, on 1 August 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/weather/uae-weather-temperatures-hit-518cin-al-ain-says-ncm), the highest temperature of that year.

Heat is only part of the picture. According to NCM, [the average maximum relative humidity in August is 72 per cent](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/environment/why-does-it-feel-so-much-hotter-humidity-in-the-uae-explained-1.884372). Your body cools itself mainly by sweating, and sweat only cools you when it evaporates. In humid air, less of it evaporates, so your temperature can keep climbing while you are soaked. Once the wet-bulb temperature (a measure that combines heat and humidity) goes above 35°C, [even fit people cannot cool themselves by sweating](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/environment/why-does-it-feel-so-much-hotter-humidity-in-the-uae-explained-1.884372).

Awareness is patchy. In a [survey of 402 adults in four UAE cities](https://ijms.info/IJMS/article/view/1269), 32% did not know that severe heat stroke can cause death.

## Dehydration symptoms

Dehydration means your body has lost more fluid than it has taken in. The [NHS lists these signs](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/) in adults and children:

- feeling thirsty
- dark yellow, strong-smelling urine
- peeing less often than usual
- headache, dizziness or light-headedness
- feeling tired
- dry mouth, lips and tongue
- sunken eyes

In babies, look for [a sunken soft spot on the head (fontanelle), few or no tears when crying, fewer wet nappies, and drowsiness or irritability](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/).

Mild dehydration usually settles with regular drinking until your urine is pale. Your pharmacist can recommend [oral rehydration solution](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/) if vomiting or diarrhoea is causing you to lose a lot of fluid. Do not give young children [fruit juice or fizzy drinks, as these can make diarrhoea or vomiting worse](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/).

Get urgent help if someone who is dehydrated has [pale or blotchy skin, cold skin, difficulty breathing, confusion, or is very drowsy](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/). These can be signs of shock.

## Heat exhaustion

Heat exhaustion happens when the body starts to struggle with heat. Typical [heat exhaustion symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) are tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea or vomiting, heavy sweating, pale and clammy skin, cramps, a fast pulse or breathing, thirst and irritability. [CDC/NIOSH](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html) adds weakness and passing less urine.

The key point is that the person is still thinking clearly.

What to do, [following NHS advice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/):

1. Move them to a cool place, ideally shade or air conditioning.
2. Remove any extra clothing.
3. Give plenty of water, or a rehydration drink or sports drink.
4. Cool the skin by spraying or sponging with cool water, or put cold packs on the armpits and neck.

They should start to feel better within 30 minutes. If they do not, or if they develop confusion, a seizure, a very high temperature, fast breathing or a fast heartbeat, [call 998](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies).

### Heat cramps

Heat cramps are [painful spasms in the abdomen, arms or legs](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html). CDC/NIOSH advises drinking water with carbohydrate and electrolyte replacement every 15 to 20 minutes, and avoiding salt tablets. Get medical help if you have heart problems, are on a low-sodium diet, or the cramps last longer than an hour.

## Heat stroke symptoms

Heat stroke is [a severe rise in body temperature, usually above 40°C, together with signs that the brain is affected](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537135/), such as unsteadiness, delirium or seizures. [CDC/NIOSH lists](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html) confusion, slurred speech, loss of consciousness, seizures and very high body temperature. Body temperature can climb to 106°F (41°C) or higher within 10 to 15 minutes, and heat stroke can be fatal without treatment.

There are two types, and the difference matters:

- **Classic heat stroke** tends to affect [older people with long-term conditions](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537135/) and the skin is usually hot and dry. A UAE emergency consultant also includes [young children, people with other illnesses and people on certain medications](https://ssmc.ae/news/how-to-beat-heatstroke-and-stay-safe-in-uae-summer/).
- **Exertional heat stroke** affects otherwise healthy people working or exercising in heat, and they [usually keep sweating](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537135/).

So sweating does not rule out heat stroke. CDC/NIOSH describes the skin in heat stroke as [hot and dry, or sweating heavily](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html). Confusion or a change in behaviour in someone who is hot is the sign to act on.

### What to do while waiting for the ambulance

1. **Call 998.** It is the [UAE ambulance number and works from anywhere in the country](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies).
2. **Stop all activity and move the person out of the heat.** Take off outer clothing.
3. **Cool them fast, starting now.** [Cooling should begin at the scene](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537135/) and should not be delayed until transport arrives.
4. **Use the best cooling method you have.** [Sheikh Shakhbout Medical City in Abu Dhabi](https://ssmc.ae/news/how-to-beat-heatstroke-and-stay-safe-in-uae-summer/) ranks the options in this order. Cold water immersion is fastest. Next is spraying the skin with water and fanning. Then ice packs or cooling blankets. If you cannot immerse the person, [put cold, wet cloths or ice on the head, neck, armpits and groin](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html) and keep wetting and fanning the skin.
5. **Keep cooling** and follow the instructions of the ambulance call-taker.

Watch for [dark, tea- or cola-coloured urine](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html) with muscle pain or weakness after hard work or exercise in the heat. This can be rhabdomyolysis, a breakdown of muscle. Stop, drink water and get medical care straight away.

## ORS vs water vs sports drinks

**Water** is enough for everyday hydration. [Harvard Health notes](https://www.health.harvard.edu/diet-and-nutrition/gatorade-liquid-iv-do-you-need-extra-electrolytes) that plain water is fine during or after moderate activity lasting under an hour.

**Oral rehydration salts (ORS)** are the right choice when you are losing fluid and salts quickly, for example with vomiting or diarrhoea, or when you have dehydration symptoms after heat exposure. The WHO and UNICEF reduced-osmolarity formula is a precise mix of sugar and salts, with [an osmolarity of 245 mOsm/L, 75 mmol/L of sodium and 75 mmol/L of glucose](https://wikem.org/wiki/Reduced-osmolarity_oral_rehydration_solution). Buy ready-made sachets from a pharmacy and mix them exactly as the pack says.

**Sports or electrolyte drinks** make more sense for [high-intensity activity lasting an hour or longer, a physically demanding workday, heavy sweating, or exercise in hot or humid weather](https://www.health.harvard.edu/diet-and-nutrition/gatorade-liquid-iv-do-you-need-extra-electrolytes). Many contain a lot of sugar, sweeteners and colourings, and habitual use may not suit people with diabetes or high blood pressure.

**Fizzy drinks and fruit juice** are poor choices when a child has diarrhoea or vomiting, because they [can make it worse](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/).

If you have heart or kidney problems, or you have been told to limit salt or fluids, ask your pharmacist or doctor before using ORS or electrolyte drinks.

## Who is at higher risk

The [NHS names children, older people and people with long-term conditions such as diabetes or heart problems](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) as higher risk. A 2026 review of heat emergencies in the Gulf found that heat illness [disproportionately affects outdoor workers, pilgrims, older adults and people with chronic disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42634775/). People who train hard outdoors and people who are not used to the heat are also exposed, since [lack of acclimatisation](https://ssmc.ae/news/how-to-beat-heatstroke-and-stay-safe-in-uae-summer/) contributes to exertional heat stroke.

### Outdoor workers and the Midday Break

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation's Midday Break ran from [15 June to 15 September 2026, between 12:30 pm and 3:00 pm](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/uae/uaes-annual-midday-break-returns-what-workers-and-employers-need-to-know/), for work under direct sunlight and in open areas. 2026 was its 22nd consecutive year.

During this period employers must provide [shaded rest areas, fans or other cooling, enough drinking water, hydration supplements including salts, and first-aid equipment](https://sharjah24.ae/en/Articles/2026/06/08/KMR16). Violations can be reported to MoHRE on [600590000](https://sharjah24.ae/en/Articles/2026/06/08/KMR16) or through its website and app.

The break covers only 12:30 to 3:00 pm. Heat illness can still happen in the morning or evening, especially on humid days, so the same warning signs apply at any hour.

Avoiding the midday sun is sensible in summer. If you stay out of the sun most of the year, our guide to [vitamin D deficiency in the UAE](/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-uae/) is worth a read.

## Medicines that can raise heat risk

The [CDC's 2025 guidance for clinicians](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/hcp/clinical-guidance/heat-and-medications-guidance-for-clinicians.html) lists medicines that may increase the risk of heat illness:

- **Diuretics (water tablets):** fluid and salt loss, reduced thirst
- **Beta-blockers:** less sweating and less widening of blood vessels in the skin
- **ACE inhibitors and ARBs:** lower blood pressure, reduced thirst
- **Lithium:** fluid and salt imbalance, and a higher risk of toxicity
- **Antipsychotics:** impaired sweating and temperature control
- **Antidepressants:** SSRIs and SNRIs can increase sweating, and tricyclics can reduce it
- **Stimulants such as methylphenidate:** higher body temperature
- **Antihistamines with anticholinergic effects:** less sweating
- **Some antiepileptics, including topiramate:** less sweating
- **NSAID painkillers and aspirin:** a higher risk of kidney injury when you are dehydrated

The research behind this list is mixed. A [2024 meta-analysis of 35 studies with 353 participants](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(24)00465-6/fulltext) found measurable rises in core temperature during heat stress only with strong anticholinergics (the evidence was limited to atropine), non-selective beta-blockers, adrenaline and anti-Parkinson medicines. The rises were small, between about 0.1°C and 0.4°C. Antidepressants and diuretics showed no significant effect on core temperature. The studies included few women, older adults or people with chronic disease.

This fits with the CDC list. For diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs and NSAIDs, the main concern is dehydration and strain on the kidneys.

**Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own.** The CDC advises clinicians to remind patients [not to stop any medicine abruptly without a plan](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/hcp/clinical-guidance/heat-and-medications-guidance-for-clinicians.html). If you take any of these, ask your pharmacist how to stay safe in the heat, especially if you take several at once.

## Keeping medicines safe in the heat

The [CDC advises](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/hcp/clinical-guidance/heat-and-medications-guidance-for-clinicians.html) never leaving medicines in a car or anywhere else that gets very hot. It highlights three risks:

- **Insulin** should be refrigerated and may become less effective if left in the heat for long periods.
- **Inhalers** can burst in hot places such as a car boot.
- **Adrenaline auto-injectors** may malfunction or deliver less adrenaline after heat exposure.

Store medicines as the label says, and never leave them in a parked car. Carry insulin, inhalers and auto-injectors with you. If a medicine has been left somewhere hot, ask your pharmacist whether it is still safe to use.

## How much water you actually need

The origin of the "8 glasses a day" rule is [not clear](https://www.health.harvard.edu/digestive-health/the-8-glasses-per-day-rule). Most people with access to water get enough from what they normally drink and eat. Food counts too.

The European Food Safety Authority considers [2.0 litres a day adequate for women and 2.5 litres for men](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/press/news/nda100326). That is total water from food and drink combined. In UAE heat, and when you are active, you will need more.

Instead of counting glasses, use these simple checks:

- Drink regularly through the day.
- Check your urine. [Aim for a pale colour](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/).
- During exercise, [drink to thirst](https://sportsmedicine.mayoclinic.org/news/drink-to-thirst-when-exercising/).
- If you take a medicine that can [reduce thirst](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/hcp/clinical-guidance/heat-and-medications-guidance-for-clinicians.html), such as a diuretic, ACE inhibitor or ARB, use urine colour as a backup.

More is not always better. Drinking far too much, especially plain water during long exercise, can [lower blood sodium](https://sportsmedicine.mayoclinic.org/news/drink-to-thirst-when-exercising/). Severe cases can cause [brain swelling, confusion, seizures or coma](https://www.health.harvard.edu/digestive-health/the-8-glasses-per-day-rule), although this is hard to do in everyday life.

## Questions people ask

**Can you have heat stroke if you are still sweating?**
Yes. People with exertional heat stroke [usually keep sweating](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK537135/). Confusion or strange behaviour in the heat matters more than dry skin.

**How long does heat exhaustion take to get better?**
Someone with heat exhaustion should [start to feel better within 30 minutes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) of resting somewhere cool. If they do not, call 998.

**What is the ambulance number in the UAE?**
[998](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies). Police is 999 and fire is 997. All work from anywhere in the UAE.

_This article is general information. If someone is confused, collapses or has a seizure in the heat, call 998 immediately._
