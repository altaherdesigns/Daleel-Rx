A lot of pharmacists in the UAE still say "MOHAP" by habit when they mean the drug regulator. That's understandable — it was true for years. It stopped being true on 2 January 2025.

## What changed

Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024 replaced Federal Law No. 8 of 2019 on medical products, the pharmacy profession, and pharmaceutical establishments. It created a new federal body, the **Emirates Drug Establishment (EDE)**, and moved the functions that used to sit inside the Ministry of Health and Prevention's drug directorate across to it.

EDE is now the one issuing marketing authorisations for medical products, running pharmacovigilance and market surveillance, licensing pharmaceutical establishments, and overseeing advertising and import of medical products at the federal level. Before this, some of that authority was split across different departments and processes. The new law consolidates it into a single body.

## What didn't move

Emirate-level healthcare regulation is unchanged. DHA still regulates healthcare in Dubai, SHA in Sharjah, and MOHAP still functions as the health authority for the Northern Emirates on matters outside drug registration — professional licensing among them. What moved to EDE specifically is the *federal drug regulatory function*: whether a product can be marketed in the UAE at all, regardless of which emirate a pharmacy sits in.

## Why it matters day to day

If you're chasing a registration query, a marketing authorisation status, or anything about whether a product is even legally on the market, EDE is the address now — not MOHAP's old drug directorate. Products registered before the transition keep their historical registration record; nothing here retroactively changes what's already approved. But the front door changed, and a fair number of directories, forms, and habits haven't caught up yet.
