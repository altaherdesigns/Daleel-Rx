Ask a pharmacist moving between emirates which licence they need, and the answer isn't
"a UAE pharmacy licence" — it's four separate answers depending on where the job actually is.

## Four authorities, four jurisdictions

The UAE runs healthcare licensing through four separate regulators, each covering its own
patch:

- **DHA** (Dubai Health Authority) — Dubai
- **DOH** (Department of Health) — Abu Dhabi
- **SHA** (Sharjah Health Authority) — Sharjah
- **MOHAP** (Ministry of Health and Prevention) — the remaining Northern Emirates:
  Ajman, Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah, and Umm Al Quwain

None of them licenses you to practise in another's jurisdiction. A DHA licence lets you
work in Dubai — not Sharjah, not Abu Dhabi, not Fujairah.

## Same framework, separate processes

All four authorities issue licences under a jointly published document — the Unified
Healthcare Professional Qualification Requirements (PQR) — so the underlying bar is
broadly consistent: a recognised pharmacy degree, primary source verification of your
credentials through DataFlow, and a minimum period of post-qualification experience.

What isn't shared is the process itself. Each authority runs its own application portal,
sets its own exam, and issues its own licence number. Passing DHA's exam doesn't register
you with MOHAP, and vice versa — you're applying to four distinct systems that happen to
agree on the underlying qualification standard, not one system with four doors.

## What this means in practice

Where you're licensed to work follows the job, not your home address or where you
trained. A pharmacist living in Sharjah but working in a Dubai pharmacy needs a DHA
licence, not an SHA one.

It's also common for pharmacists to end up holding more than one licence over a career —
typically starting with whichever authority matches their first job, then adding a second
once they move. Some licensing consultants describe a faster path for that second licence
once you've built practical UAE experience under the first, with your DataFlow report
carried over rather than repeated. That detail varies enough by case that it's worth
confirming directly with the authority you're applying to rather than assuming it applies
to you — the PQR document itself is the primary reference, and the specific waiver terms
are the kind of detail that changes without much notice.

## The practical takeaway

If you're planning a move between emirates, check the destination authority's own
portal before assuming your current licence — or even your current employer's
paperwork — carries over. It usually doesn't, and finding that out after accepting an
offer is a worse time to learn it.
